Shelf 1: Process Safety Management Core Texts

  • Process Safety Management by Ian Sutton – PSM Program GuideIan Sutton · Elsevier · 2025

    Ian Sutton's Process Safety Management is a practical, single-author tour of every PSM element, from process safety information and PHA to MOC, mechanical integrity and audits. It reads like advice from a practitioner who has run these programs, which makes it a strong first book for engineers new to OSHA 1910.119 or anyone building a PSM program from scratch. Keep it within reach as the 'big picture' reference that ties the specialist CCPS guidelines together.

  • CCPS Guidelines for Risk Based Process Safety (RBPS 20 Elements)Center for Chemical Process Safety (CCPS) · Wiley

    Guidelines for Risk Based Process Safety is the CCPS framework most modern PSM programs are built on. It organizes process safety into four pillars and twenty elements, then explains how to scale each element to the actual risk at your site instead of applying a one-size-fits-all checklist. If you only own one CCPS book, many practitioners would make it this one. It is essential reading for PSM coordinators, auditors and anyone preparing for a process safety leadership role.

  • Guidelines for Implementing Process Safety Management Systems (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS classic focuses on the 'how' of standing up a process safety management system: assigning accountability, sequencing implementation, setting up documentation and keeping momentum after the first rollout. It predates RBPS, but its implementation advice is still highly practical for small and mid-sized facilities that need a realistic roadmap. It is a useful companion for engineers inheriting a PSM program that exists on paper but is not yet working in the field.

  • Guidelines for Auditing Process Safety Management Systems, 2nd EditionCCPS · Wiley · 2022

    The definitive CCPS guide to planning and running a PSM compliance or management-system audit. It covers audit protocols, sampling, interviewing, evaluating evidence and writing findings that actually drive corrective action, with element-by-element questions mapped to OSHA PSM and EPA RMP. Whether you are leading a three-year compliance audit or preparing your unit for one, this is the book auditors themselves use. It is highly recommended for PSM coordinators and process safety engineers.

  • Plant Guidelines for Technical Management of Chemical Process SafetyCCPS · Wiley

    Written for plant-level engineers and managers, this CCPS title turns process safety management into day-to-day technical practice: process knowledge, hazard reviews, change control, training and incident follow-up at the unit level. It is less about corporate policy and more about what a site technical team does every week to keep hazards controlled. It is a solid desk reference for operations and technical-services engineers who want the plant view of PSM rather than the boardroom view.

  • Process Safety for Engineers: An Introduction, 2nd Edition (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2022

    Process Safety for Engineers is the CCPS introduction aimed at early-career and non-specialist engineers. It explains hazards, consequences, PSM elements and lessons from major incidents in plain language, with examples drawn from real facilities. It is ideal for new graduates, project engineers rotating into operations, or anyone who needs a fast, credible grounding before diving into the detailed Guidelines series. I recommend it as the first CCPS book for most engineers.

  • Conduct of Operations and Operational Discipline – CCPS Process Safety BookCCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS book tackles the human side of running a plant well: doing the right task, the right way, every time. It defines conduct of operations and operational discipline, shows how they reinforce procedures and safety culture, and gives practical tools such as shift turnover, control-room behaviors and field verification. It is a must-read for operations supervisors, plant managers and process safety engineers trying to close the gap between written procedures and actual practice.

  • Guidelines for Process Safety Knowledge Management (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2024

    Process safety depends on finding the right information at the right time, and this CCPS guideline shows how to capture, organize and protect that knowledge. It covers process safety information, document control, retiring-workforce knowledge loss and digital tools for keeping engineering data current. It is especially valuable for facilities with aging staff or legacy documentation, and for engineers responsible for PSI accuracy ahead of PHAs and audits.

  • Guidelines for Managing Abnormal Situations (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2022

    Most serious incidents start as an abnormal situation that was not recognized or handled in time. This CCPS guideline explains how to anticipate, detect and respond to upsets, covering alarm management, operator decision-making, procedures for non-routine modes and organizational readiness. It is practical reading for control-room supervisors, operations engineers and process safety professionals who want to strengthen the barrier between a process deviation and a loss of containment.

  • Process Safety in Upstream Oil and Gas (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2021

    This CCPS title adapts process safety principles to wells, production facilities, offshore platforms and midstream gathering, where conditions differ sharply from refineries and chemical plants. It addresses remote and unmanned sites, contractor-heavy workforces and well-control hazards within a risk-based PSM framework. It is highly relevant for engineers in exploration and production, and for downstream professionals moving into upstream roles who need to understand what changes and what does not.

  • Guidelines for Process Safety in Outsourced Manufacturing (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2026

    When a toll manufacturer or contract producer handles your chemistry, the process safety risk does not leave with the purchase order. This CCPS guideline explains how to select, qualify and monitor outsourced manufacturing partners, how to transfer process knowledge and how to share responsibility for hazards. It is useful for specialty chemical, pharmaceutical and consumer-product companies, and for engineers who manage supplier relationships.

    1st edition (2000), 'Outsourced Manufacturing Operations'
  • Driving Continuous Process Safety Improvement From Investigated IncidentsCCPS · Wiley · 2021

    Investigations only matter if their lessons change how a facility operates. This CCPS book focuses on the step after the report: tracking recommendations, sharing lessons across sites, spotting trends in near misses and measuring whether improvements stick. It connects incident investigation to management review and safety culture. It is a strong pick for PSM leaders and investigation team members who want their organization to stop repeating the same incidents.

  • Guidelines for Implementing Process Safety Management, 2nd EditionCCPS · Wiley

    The second edition updates CCPS's implementation guidance for the risk-based era, walking through how to plan, resource, roll out and sustain each PSM element. It includes practical examples for organizations of different sizes and maturity levels and connects implementation to RBPS. It is a valuable roadmap for new PSM managers, companies expanding into regulated processes, or sites rebuilding a program after an audit finding or incident.

  • Guidelines for Integrating Management Systems and Metrics to Improve Process Safety PerformanceCCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline shows how to tie process safety into the management systems a company already runs, including quality, environmental, occupational safety and business planning, and how to measure performance with meaningful leading and lagging metrics. It helps avoid parallel, competing programs. It is useful for corporate EHS leaders and site managers who want process safety to be part of how the business is run, not a separate binder.

  • Guidelines for Defining Process Safety Competency Requirements (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Who needs to know what about process safety, and how well? This CCPS guideline gives a structured way to define competencies by role, from operators to executives, assess gaps and build training and qualification programs that hold up to scrutiny. It is excellent for training coordinators, PSM managers and engineering leaders building career ladders, and for any engineer mapping their own professional development.

  • Guidelines for Process Safety Documentation (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Good documentation is the foundation for every PHA, MOC and audit. This CCPS guideline explains which process safety records to keep, how to organize them, how long to retain them and how to keep them current as the plant changes. It is an older title, but its principles for managing PSI, procedures and design records remain sound. It is useful for document-control specialists and engineers responsible for audit readiness.

  • Guidelines for the Management of Change for Process Safety (CCPS MOC Book)CCPS · Wiley

    Management of change is one of the most frequently cited PSM elements in incident reports and audits. This CCPS guideline explains how to design an MOC system that works: defining what counts as a change, risk-ranking reviews, handling temporary and organizational changes, and closing out properly. It is practical and example-driven, and I would call it essential for anyone who approves, reviews or administers MOCs at an operating facility.

  • Contractor and Client Relations to Assure Process Safety (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Contractors perform much of the high-hazard work at process plants, and this CCPS title addresses how owners and contractors share process safety responsibilities. It covers contractor selection, pre-job planning, hazard communication, oversight during turnarounds and performance evaluation. It is a helpful reference for maintenance and turnaround planners, contractor safety coordinators and engineers who supervise contract crews in covered processes.

Shelf 2: Hazard Analysis & Risk Assessment

  • Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures – HAZOP, What-If, FMEA (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Often called the 'HEP book', this is the standard reference for process hazard analysis methods: HAZOP, What-If, checklists, FMEA, fault tree and event tree, with worked examples for each. It explains how to choose a method, prepare a team, run sessions and document results. Anyone who facilitates, scribes or participates in PHAs should own this book. It is the single most-used CCPS guideline in many process safety departments.

    Newer 3rd edition (2008), 'Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures'
  • Layer of Protection Analysis (LOPA) – Simplified Process Risk Assessment (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This is the original CCPS book that defined LOPA, the semi-quantitative method used worldwide to decide whether safeguards are enough and to set SIL targets for safety instrumented functions. It explains initiating events, independent protection layers, frequencies and risk tolerance with clear worked examples. It is essential reading for PHA facilitators, SIS engineers and anyone who signs off on LOPA worksheets. Pair it with the IE/IPL and enabling-conditions guidelines.

  • Guidelines for Enabling Conditions and Conditional Modifiers in LOPACCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline tackles one of the most misused parts of LOPA: enabling conditions and conditional modifiers such as probability of ignition, occupancy and time at risk. It explains when these factors are legitimate, how to justify the values and how to avoid stacking optimistic credits. It is a highly practical companion to the core LOPA book for facilitators and reviewers who want defensible, consistent risk assessments.

  • Guidelines for Developing Quantitative Safety Risk Criteria (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    How safe is safe enough? This CCPS guideline explains how companies set individual and societal risk criteria, F-N curves and risk matrices, and how those criteria are used in LOPA and QRA. It reviews international benchmarks and the ethical and practical choices behind them. It is valuable for risk analysts, PSM managers and anyone tasked with building or defending a corporate risk-tolerance standard.

  • Bow-Tie Industrial Risk Management Across Sectors by Luca FiorentiniLuca Fiorentini · Wiley · 2021

    Luca Fiorentini's book is a practical guide to barrier-based risk management with bow-tie diagrams, drawing examples from process industries, energy, transportation and other sectors. It covers building bow ties, assessing barrier quality, linking barriers to management systems and using them in incident analysis. It is a great choice for engineers who want to communicate risk visually to operators and management, and for teams adopting bow ties alongside HAZOP and LOPA.

  • A Practical Approach to Hazard Identification for Operations and Maintenance Workers (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS book brings hazard recognition to the people closest to the equipment. It gives operators and maintenance technicians simple, field-ready ways to spot hazards before a job, during routine rounds and when something looks 'not quite right', using real incident examples. It is excellent for frontline training programs and for engineers who coach field teams. It helps turn process safety from an engineering exercise into an everyday habit.

  • Revalidating Process Hazard Analyses (CCPS, 1st Edition)CCPS · Wiley

    OSHA requires PHAs to be updated and revalidated at least every five years, and this original CCPS guide explains how to do it efficiently. It covers evaluating the prior study, deciding between an update and a full redo, and incorporating changes and incidents since the last review. It is a useful historical reference; most readers will want the second edition, but this version still offers clear reasoning on revalidation scope.

  • Guidelines for Revalidating a Process Hazard Analysis, 2nd Edition (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2022

    The second edition is the current CCPS reference for five-year PHA revalidations. It provides a structured approach to judging the quality of the previous PHA, choosing between retain, redo and update, folding in MOCs and incident history, and documenting the decision. It includes checklists and examples. It is strongly recommended for PHA facilitators and PSM coordinators who schedule and lead revalidations.

  • Human Factors Handbook for Process Plant Operations (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2022

    This CCPS handbook is a practical, plant-focused guide to human factors: workload, fatigue, control-room design, alarm handling, procedures, communication and human error. It is organized so engineers and supervisors can quickly find guidance on specific issues and apply it during design reviews, PHAs and investigations. It is one of the most useful modern CCPS titles for anyone who wants to design systems around how people actually work.

  • Handbook for Process Safety in Laboratories and Pilot Plants (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2023

    Laboratories and pilot plants handle new chemistry, small scales and frequent changes, all conditions where traditional PSM tools can fall short. This CCPS handbook adapts risk-based process safety to R&D settings, covering hazard identification, chemical reactivity screening, MOC for experiments and scale-up. It is ideal for R&D engineers, university lab managers and EHS staff supporting research and pilot facilities.

  • Guidelines for Initiating Events and Independent Protection Layers in LOPACCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline is the go-to source for defensible LOPA data. It provides recommended initiating-event frequencies and IPL probabilities of failure on demand, along with the criteria an IPL must meet to be credited. It explains how to validate site-specific values. If you facilitate or review LOPAs, this book belongs next to the original LOPA text and will make your analyses more consistent and auditable.

  • Bow Ties in Risk Management: A Concept Book for Process Safety (CCPS & Energy Institute)CCPS · Wiley

    Produced by CCPS with the Energy Institute, this concept book sets out a consistent approach to building and using bow-tie diagrams in process safety. It covers threats, consequences, prevention and mitigation barriers, degradation factors and barrier management, with clear rules that prevent common bow-tie mistakes. It is a concise, authoritative reference for teams introducing bow ties or standardizing how they are drawn across sites.

  • Guide for Making Acute Risk Decisions (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guide addresses a hard question: how to make sound decisions about low-frequency, high-consequence risks when data are uncertain and stakeholders disagree. It introduces structured decision-making approaches and explains how to weigh risk, cost and other factors transparently. It is useful background for risk analysts and managers who must justify risk-reduction investments or choose between design alternatives.

  • Tools for Making Acute Risk Decisions with Chemical Process Safety Applications (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS book goes beyond the concepts and provides practical decision-analysis tools, such as cost-benefit methods, multi-attribute analysis and decision trees, applied to chemical process safety problems. It shows how to structure options, quantify trade-offs and document the rationale. It is a useful reference for engineers and analysts who need to move from 'what is the risk?' to 'what should we do about it?'

  • Biological Safety: Principles and Practices, 5th Edition (ASM Press)Dawn P. Wooley & Karen B. Byers (eds.) · ASM Press · 2017

    Since no book exists under the bioprocess hazard-identification title on your list, this is the closest authoritative reference. Edited by Dawn Wooley and Karen Byers, the ASM Press handbook covers how to identify, assess and control biological risks in laboratories, production facilities and classrooms, plus biorisk management, regulations and containment. Pair it with CCPS's bioprocess manufacturing guideline (#39) to cover both the biological and the chemical/physical hazards of fermentation, cell culture and biopharma operations.

Shelf 3: Engineering Design & Facility Safety

  • Guidelines for Engineering Design for Process Safety, 2nd Edition (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline is a comprehensive design reference that shows how process safety is built into equipment, piping, layout, relief, controls and utilities from the start. It links design decisions to the hazards they control and to codes and standards, with an inherently safer design mindset throughout. It is essential for design engineers, project engineers and anyone reviewing designs during FEED, detailed engineering or MOC.

  • Guidelines for Facility Siting and Layout, 2nd Edition (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2022

    Where you put equipment and buildings determines how bad a fire, explosion or toxic release can become. This CCPS guideline covers spacing, layout principles, occupied-building placement and siting studies, and connects them to consequence and risk analysis. It is valuable for project engineers, layout designers and process safety engineers doing facility siting studies under API RP 752 and 753 or similar requirements.

    Earlier 1st edition (2003), 'Guidelines for Facility Siting and Layout'
  • Guidelines for Evaluating Process Plant Buildings for External Explosions, Fires, and Toxic ReleasesCCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline is the detailed reference for building siting evaluations. It explains how to assess occupied and unoccupied buildings against blast overpressure, fire and toxic gas ingress, set occupancy criteria and choose mitigation such as relocation or blast-resistant design. It is highly recommended for engineers responsible for API RP 752/753 compliance, control-room upgrades and new-building placement.

  • Guidelines for Performing Effective Pre-Startup Safety Reviews (PSSR) – CCPSCCPS · Wiley

    The pre-startup safety review is the last check before hazardous materials are introduced, and this CCPS guideline explains how to do it well. It covers PSSR scope, team makeup, checklists, verifying that PHA recommendations and procedures are complete, and handling punch-list items. It is practical and checklist-rich, and it is ideal for project engineers, operations leads and PSM coordinators who sign off on startups.

  • Guidelines for Process Safety Metrics (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    You cannot manage what you do not measure. This CCPS guideline explains how to choose leading and lagging process safety indicators, collect consistent data and use metrics to drive improvement rather than just report numbers. It aligns with industry Tier 1 and Tier 2 event definitions. It is a strong pick for PSM managers and site leaders building a process safety dashboard or KPI program.

  • Guidelines for Process Safety in Bioprocess Manufacturing Facilities (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline applies the full range of process safety management to bioprocess manufacturing, including fermentation, cell culture, purification and the utilities that support them. It addresses biological, chemical and physical hazards together and adapts PSM elements to GMP-regulated environments. It is useful for engineers in biopharma, biofuels and industrial biotech who need a process safety framework that fits their facilities.

  • Process Plant Design for Chemical Engineers by Peter MullingerPeter Mullinger · Elsevier Butterworth-Heinemann · 2025

    Peter Mullinger's book focuses on the practical engineering decisions that turn a process flowsheet into a buildable, operable and safe plant: equipment selection, layout, utilities, materials, costing and the trade-offs between them. It reads like a senior engineer explaining what textbooks leave out. It is an excellent bridge for new chemical engineers moving into design and project roles, and a useful refresher for experienced engineers mentoring others.

  • Guidelines for Design Solutions for Process Equipment Failures (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline catalogs common equipment failure scenarios for reactors, columns, vessels, heat exchangers, pumps and piping, and pairs them with inherent, passive, active and procedural design solutions. It is extremely handy during PHAs and design reviews when the team asks, 'What else could we do here?' It is a practical reference for design engineers, PHA facilitators and anyone developing safeguard recommendations.

  • Guidelines for Integrating Process Safety into Engineering Projects (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Process safety is cheapest to get right early. This CCPS guideline shows how to embed hazard reviews, inherently safer design, siting, SIS and PSSR activities into each phase of a capital project, from concept through startup. It includes project-stage checklists and deliverables. It is ideal for project managers, project engineers and process safety leads on capital projects of any size.

Shelf 4: Fire Protection & Explosion Safety

  • Guidelines for Fire Protection in Chemical, Petrochemical, and Hydrocarbon Processing FacilitiesCCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline covers the full fire protection picture for process plants: fire hazard analysis, passive protection, fixed water and foam systems, detection, firewater supply and emergency response interfaces. It connects fire protection design to process hazards and NFPA and API practices. It is a key reference for fire protection engineers, process safety engineers and anyone performing fire hazard analyses at hydrocarbon facilities.

  • Explosion Safety for Industries: Prevention and Protection by Estellito Rangel JuniorEstellito Rangel Junior · CRC Press · 2025

    Estellito Rangel Junior's book offers a broad, industry-oriented guide to explosion prevention and protection, covering flammable gases, vapors and dusts, hazardous area classification, ignition sources and protection methods such as venting, suppression and isolation. It is a recent, practical overview for engineers who need to understand explosion risk across different industries. It is a good complement to the more specialized CCPS explosion titles.

  • Understanding Explosions – CCPS Guide to Explosion HazardsCCPS · Wiley

    Understanding Explosions is a readable CCPS introduction to explosion phenomena: vapor cloud explosions, BLEVEs, dust explosions, runaway reactions and physical explosions. It explains the science in accessible terms and illustrates it with incident examples, then outlines prevention and mitigation strategies. It is ideal for engineers, operators and managers who need a solid conceptual foundation before tackling detailed consequence modeling.

  • Guidelines for Vapor Cloud Explosion, Pressure Vessel Burst, BLEVE, and Flash Fire HazardsCCPS · Wiley · 2021

    This CCPS guideline is the technical reference for estimating blast and fire consequences from VCEs, pressure vessel bursts, BLEVEs and flash fires. It reviews prediction methods such as TNO multi-energy and Baker-Strehlow-Tang, their assumptions and limitations, with worked examples. It is essential for consequence modelers, facility siting analysts and process safety engineers who must defend explosion overpressure estimates.

  • Avoiding Static Ignition Hazards in Chemical Operations (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Static electricity is a frequent but often overlooked ignition source in chemical handling. This CCPS concept book explains how static charge is generated and discharged in liquids, powders and gases, and gives practical guidance on bonding, grounding, conductive equipment and safe transfer operations. It is a concise, practical read for operations, maintenance and process engineers who handle flammable liquids or combustible powders.

  • Deflagration and Detonation Flame Arresters (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Flame arresters are deceptively simple devices that are easy to misapply. This CCPS guideline explains how deflagration and detonation arresters work, how to select and locate them, the relevant test standards and the inspection and maintenance needed to keep them effective. It is a valuable reference for engineers designing vent systems, vapor recovery and tank protection, and for those auditing existing installations.

  • Guidelines for Determining the Probability of Ignition of a Released Flammable Mass (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    How likely is a flammable release to ignite? This CCPS guideline provides a structured method for estimating ignition probability based on release size, material properties and ignition sources, and explains how to use the results in LOPA and QRA. It replaces rule-of-thumb guesses with defensible values. It is a must-have for risk analysts and LOPA facilitators who apply probability of ignition as a conditional modifier.

  • Dust Explosions in the Process Industries by Rolf Eckhoff, 3rd EditionRolf K. Eckhoff · Gulf Professional Publishing · 2003

    Rolf Eckhoff's book is the classic technical reference on dust explosions: ignition sensitivity, explosion violence, propagation in equipment and buildings, and the prevention and mitigation measures (venting, suppression, isolation, inerting) that a risk-based dust program relies on. It is detailed and data-rich, with extensive incident history. Read it alongside CCPS's Combustible Dust Hazard Analysis guideline (#52) when you need to understand why the NFPA requirements are written the way they are.

    Also good: An Introduction to Dust Explosions (Paul Amyotte)
  • Electrostatic Ignitions of Fires and Explosions (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This in-depth CCPS reference explores the physics of electrostatic charging and discharge and how it ignites flammable atmospheres in industrial operations. It covers charge generation in liquids, powders and gases, types of discharges, and engineering controls, with case studies. It is a more technical complement to Avoiding Static Ignition Hazards and is ideal for engineers investigating static-related incidents or designing high-risk transfer systems.

  • Guidelines for Combustible Dust Hazard Analysis (DHA) – CCPSCCPS · Wiley

    NFPA 652 requires a dust hazard analysis for facilities handling combustible dust, and this CCPS guideline explains how to perform one. It covers dust characterization, identifying fire and explosion scenarios, evaluating safeguards and documenting the DHA, with worked examples. It is highly practical for engineers, EHS managers and consultants responsible for DHA compliance in food, agricultural, chemical, metal and wood-products facilities.

Shelf 5: Mechanical Integrity & Equipment Reliability

  • Guidelines for Mechanical Integrity Systems (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Mechanical integrity is the PSM element that keeps equipment fit for service, and this CCPS guideline is the standard reference for building an MI program. It covers equipment selection, inspection and testing, quality assurance, deficiency management, training and program metrics, with guidance by equipment type. It is essential for reliability engineers, inspectors and PSM coordinators responsible for OSHA 1910.119(j) compliance.

  • Guidelines for Pressure Relief and Effluent Handling Systems (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline covers the full pressure-relief chain: identifying overpressure scenarios, sizing relief devices including two-phase flow, and designing effluent handling systems such as knockout drums, flares and scrubbers. It includes worked examples and design software guidance. It is a core reference for process design engineers, relief system specialists and anyone validating relief system design as part of PSI or MOC.

  • Guidelines for Safe Handling of Powders and Bulk Solids (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Powder handling presents hazards that liquid-focused engineers may underestimate: dust explosions, static, self-heating, flow problems and toxic exposure. This CCPS guideline covers characterizing powders and designing safe storage, conveying, milling, drying and packaging operations. It is a comprehensive reference for engineers in pharmaceuticals, food, plastics, minerals and specialty chemicals who work with solids.

  • Guidelines for Preparing Process Equipment for Maintenance and Return to Service (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley · 2025

    Many serious incidents happen during the transition into and out of maintenance. This CCPS guideline covers isolation, decontamination, draining, purging, energy control and verification before work begins, and the checks needed before equipment returns to service. It is a practical reference for operations and maintenance supervisors, turnaround planners and process safety engineers who develop or audit equipment-preparation procedures.

  • Continuous Monitoring for Hazardous Material Releases (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS title covers the design and use of continuous monitoring systems for detecting hazardous releases, including gas detector technologies, placement strategies, alarm set points and integration with emergency response. It helps engineers decide what to monitor, where and how to act on alarms. It is useful for instrument engineers, process safety engineers and emergency planners designing or upgrading detection systems.

  • Emergency Relief System Design Using DIERS Technology (CCPS/AIChE)CCPS · Wiley

    This is the classic DIERS project manual for sizing emergency relief systems for runaway reactions and two-phase flow. It explains vapor-liquid disengagement, reaction calorimetry data use and the DIERS methodology that underpins much of modern relief design. It is a specialized but essential reference for engineers sizing reactor relief or reviewing relief design for reactive systems.

  • Recognizing Catastrophic Incident Warning Signs in the Process Industries (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Major incidents rarely happen without warning signs. This CCPS book catalogs the organizational, operational and equipment red flags that preceded real catastrophes, such as overdue inspections, rising alarm counts, deferred maintenance and normalized deviations, and gives self-assessment questions for each. It is an easy-to-use, eye-opening read for managers, supervisors and engineers who want to spot trouble before it becomes an incident.

  • Guidelines for Asset Integrity Management (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline expands mechanical integrity into full lifecycle asset integrity management, integrating design, operations, inspection, maintenance and management systems. It covers risk-based inspection, integrity operating windows, deficiency management and the organizational roles needed to sustain integrity. It is a strong reference for reliability leaders, integrity engineers and PSM managers aiming to move beyond compliance-driven MI.

  • Guidelines for Process Equipment Reliability Data, with Data Tables (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline provides generic failure-rate data for process equipment, including valves, pumps, instruments, vessels and more, along with guidance on how the data were collected and how to use them appropriately. It is a long-standing source for reliability, LOPA and QRA analysts who need defensible starting values. It is a must-have reference for anyone building fault trees or estimating safeguard reliability.

  • Guidelines for Improving Plant Reliability Through Data Collection and Analysis (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS guideline explains how to collect, structure and analyze equipment failure data at your own facility so you can calculate site-specific failure rates and target reliability improvements. It describes taxonomies, data quality and analysis techniques. It is valuable for reliability engineers and process safety teams who want to replace generic data with evidence from their own operations.

  • Dealing with Aging Process Facilities and Infrastructure (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    Many process plants operate well beyond their original design life. This CCPS book addresses the hazards of aging equipment and infrastructure, covering corrosion, obsolescence, legacy documentation and knowledge loss, and offers strategies for assessing condition and prioritizing investment. It is an important read for site managers, integrity engineers and process safety professionals at older facilities.

  • Applied Metallurgy and Corrosion Control: Petrochemical Industry Handbook (Lahiri)Amiya Kumar Lahiri · Springer · 2017

    Amiya Kumar Lahiri's handbook connects metallurgy, damage mechanisms and corrosion control to materials selection decisions in refineries and petrochemical plants. It covers common degradation mechanisms, material behavior at process conditions, and practical corrosion prevention. Since no CCPS corrosion title exists under the name on your list, this is a strong practical substitute for integrity, materials and process safety engineers who want to prevent loss of containment caused by corrosion and wrong materials of construction.

Shelf 6: Chemical Reactivity & Special Hazards

  • Essential Practices for Managing Chemical Reactivity Hazards (CCPS)CCPS · Wiley

    This CCPS book offers a practical, step-by-step approach for identifying and managing chemical reactivity hazards, aimed especially at facilities that may not realize they have them.
